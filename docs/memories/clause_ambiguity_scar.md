## 2. The Crucible & The Semicolon Severance

In the commercial arbitration of *Apex Logistics v. Meridian Core*, the defense relied on Section 14.2(b), drafted as:
> *"Supplier shall indemnify Buyer against third-party claims; and losses arising from gross negligence."*

Opposing counsel persuaded the referee that the semicolon grammatically severed the clause into two standalone obligations:
1. An obligation to indemnify third-party claims.
2. A separate, **unconditioned affirmative indemnity for ANY losses**, completely untethered from gross negligence.

The referee entered an immediate $4.2M summary judgment against the client.

## 3. Operational Scars & Lessons

### Concrete Scars
- Direct $4.2M judgment entered against the client with statutory prejudgment interest.
- Eighteen months of grueling appellate review culminating in total affirmance.
- Permanent internal prohibition on semicolons in covenant drafting.

### Invariable Lessons
- Never rely on punctuation marks to limit liability; use explicit alphanumeric subclauses `(a)`, `(b)` with separate subject-verb constructions.
- Always require express bilateral written notice as a condition precedent to indemnity obligations.
