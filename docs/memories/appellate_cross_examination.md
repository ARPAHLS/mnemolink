## 2. The Crucible & Tactical Masterclass

During oral argument before a notoriously skeptical Fourth Circuit appellate panel, the presiding judge immediately confronted counsel with a procedural default in the record below. 

Rather than engaging in evasive linguistic fencing or reading canned statutory exceptions, counsel immediately conceded the procedural defect on the record:
> *"Your Honor is correct on the docket default. We do not defend it. But here is why the substantive statutory preemption under Section 12 remains fatal to their claim."*

The immediate candor disarmed the panel's hostility, pivoted the argument to the merits, and won an unanimous reversal.

---

## 3. Operational Lessons

- Never defend an indefensible procedural flaw; conceding it builds trust for the core substantive issue.
- Judicial skepticism is an invitation to demonstrate mastery, not an attack requiring defensive bluster.
